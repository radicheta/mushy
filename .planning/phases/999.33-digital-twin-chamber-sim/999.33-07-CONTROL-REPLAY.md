# MUSHY-59: control-law replay vs recorded fc.pid_output

## What this validates, and what it does not

`fc.pid_output` is recorded telemetry: the raw, pre-bias output the deployed
control law actually published (`fc_controller.py` ~line 1821,
`max(0, min(1, raw_pid_output))`). Feeding recorded `fc.humidity` into the
reconstructed law (`fc_core.sim.control_loop.ControlLoop`) and comparing its
output against that recorded value involves **no chamber physics on either
side**. A mismatch here is unambiguously a control-law reconstruction error,
not a plant-model error — that is the entire point of the ticket.

`run_closed_loop()` (`fc_core/sim/replay.py`) now calls the same
`ControlLoop` class this validator drives (MUSHY-59 refactor). There is
exactly one implementation of the law in the codebase; this validates that
implementation, not a second copy of it.

## Method summary

- Window: 2026-06-29T00:00Z .. 2026-08-09T00:00Z (41 days, 3,542,400 grid
  seconds), the span reconnaissance confirmed had a stable band
  (`[0.885, 0.915]`, `defend_side='both'`) and an unchanged deployed law.
- `fc.pid_output` and `fc.humidity_target` deduped onto a 1 Hz grid (last
  value per wall-clock second — ~48% of raw samples share a second).
  `fc.humidity` (native cadence ~2.0 s median) asof-joined onto the same
  grid, backward direction, 10 s tolerance (`GAP_BREAK_S`).
- **Driving continuity** (does the replay keep stepping one `ControlLoop`
  instance) breaks only at a real RH or target hole beyond `GAP_BREAK_S`,
  or at a restart (see below). It does NOT break at the 0.9585 ramp
  stretch, because the control law reads a **static** per-mode band
  (`BandSpec(mode.band_low, mode.band_high, mode.defend_side)`,
  `fc_controller.py` ~line 1715) — never the ramping
  `_effective_setpoint` that `fc.humidity_target` actually publishes. The
  ramp is a telemetry artifact, not a control-law input.
- **Metrics** are still excluded for every second where
  `fc.humidity_target` is not within 0.0015 of a band edge — per the brief,
  even though the law itself is unaffected — plus any second with no
  recorded `fc.pid_output` (a short ingestion gap; the loop keeps running
  through it, there is just nothing to compare against).
- **Restarts** detected independently: `fc.humidity_target > 0.92`, grouped
  into events by a >300 s gap between flagged seconds. **27 restarts** in
  the window (fully explained by the documented fc-core restart re-init bug:
  `_effective_setpoint` inits to nominal `target_humidity` (0.96) and ramps
  at `delta/30` per tick — first published tick is 0.9585 — while
  `fc_controller.__init__` simultaneously builds a fresh `PID(...,
  auto_mode=False)` with zero integral and `_d_filtered = 0.0`). A new
  `ControlLoop` is instantiated at each restart's first flagged second, even
  when the telemetry has no gap there, because the real PID state resets at
  that instant regardless of the (unaffected) band.

## Warm-up policy, and the mistake that first version made

A segment starting exactly at a restart is **restart-anchored**: the real
controller was demonstrably in the identical fresh state
(`ControlLoop.__init__` also zero-inits) at that second, so the replay uses
**zero warm-up** there. Every other segment boundary is a plain telemetry
gap with unknown mid-stream integrator state, so it gets the full 6 h
(`WARMUP_S`, ~12x the 1800 s decay time constant) discarded.

The first version of this script anchored restart segments at the END of
the excluded ramp stretch (where `fc.humidity_target` reconverges near a
band edge, ~100-150 s after the restart) and reset the PID there instead.
That silently discarded 100-150 s of real integral accumulation the actual
controller had already built up while the RH sat below the band midpoint —
a manufactured discontinuity. It surfaced exactly where predicted: the
single worst cluster of errors in the whole run (up to 0.057 absolute,
consistently *under*-predicting) landed in the tens of seconds right after
2026-06-30's restart, in `below_midpoint_feather` — precisely the regime
where integral windup matters. Anchoring at the true restart instant and
replaying continuously through the (still metrics-excluded) ramp removed
it: restart-anchored and non-anchored RMSE are now nearly identical
(0.001455 vs 0.001464 — see table below), which is the sanity check this
fix should produce if the hypothesis was right.

## Restart cost accounting

| Quantity | Value | % of window |
|---|---|---:|
| Grid seconds (41 days) | 3,542,400 | 100% |
| Real telemetry holes (RH/target gap > 10 s) | 109,157 s | 3.08% |
| Ramp/target-exclusion stretches (0.9585 anomaly + reconvergence) | 27,101 s | 0.77% |
| Segments too short to warm up (dropped entirely) | 95,291 s | 2.69% |
| **Used in metrics** | **2,991,070 s** | **84.4%** |

27 restarts detected; 25 produced an immediately-following drive-valid
stretch long enough to anchor a segment at zero warm-up (the other 2 landed
inside a telemetry hole, so their aftermath falls into an ordinary
full-warm-up segment instead). Because restart segments need zero warm-up
under the fixed policy, the "27 restarts x 6 h" worst case (~16% of the
window) never materializes — only 15 of the 98 total segments actually pay
the full 6 h warm-up, costing 90,000 s (2.5% of window) of that discard
budget; the rest of `skipped_s` is short non-restart fragments (telemetry
blips) too brief to reach 6 h even once.

## Warm-up sensitivity

RMSE as a function of warm-up length, computed over the SAME segmentation
(restart-anchored segments always use 0 regardless of this sweep — the
sweep only varies the non-restart-anchored segments' warm-up):

| Warm-up | RMSE |
|---|---|
| 1 h | 0.0015 |
| 3 h | 0.0015 |
| 6 h | 0.0015 |
| 12 h | 0.0014 |

Flat from 1 h onward. This is itself a finding: the pre-fix version showed
real sensitivity between 1 h and 3-6 h (0.0065 -> 0.0014), which was really
the restart-anchoring artifact bleeding into the sweep (short
non-restart-anchored segments were absorbing some of what should have been
restart-anchored zero-warmup time). With restarts correctly anchored, 6 h
is conservative but not load-bearing — 1 h would have been adequate for
this window's actual integrator dynamics (tau=1800 s, so 1 h is already 2x
the time constant).

| Within 0.10 | 99.9999% |
| Correlation | 0.999982 |

**Tolerance: 0.01 absolute (1 percentage point of the [0,1] duty output),
met on 99.5% of seconds.** Justification: `fc.pid_output` is itself
float32-quantized telemetry and the replay assumes exactly dt=1.0 s while
the real controller's timer jitters slightly around that value (ROS2 timer,
not a hard real-time guarantee) — sub-1% output noise from that jitter
alone is expected and not a reconstruction bug. 0.01 is roughly the level
at which "recorded vs predicted" stops being distinguishable from clock and
quantization noise, one to two orders of magnitude below the PID's actual
dynamic range of response to a feather-region error.

| freeze (`defend_side='low'` branch) | 0 | 0% | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |

`freeze` never fires: the band is `defend_side='both'` for the entire
window, and `project_error_pct` only returns `None` under `defend_side ==
'low'`. `mode_c_bypass` is a trivial perfect match by construction (both
sides just emit the literal constant 1.0 whenever RH drops more than the
5-point bypass_threshold below the defended floor — no arithmetic to get
wrong). `below_midpoint_feather` is the hardest regime, as expected: it is
where the PID actively integrates a nonzero error and small dt/timing
differences compound, but even there 97.3% of seconds are within 0.01 and
the RMSE (0.0034) stays two orders of magnitude below the branch's dynamic
range.

## Per-day metrics (sample; full table in the pickle / rerun for all 41 days)

Most days have RMSE in the 0.0000-0.003 band. Two stand out:

- **2026-07-18**: max abs error 0.1455, the single largest error in the
  dataset, at 17:04:32-38 UTC. Recorded `pid_output` is exactly 0.0000 for
  several consecutive seconds while RH sits at ~90.4% (squarely `in_band`,
  where the reconstructed law predicts ~0.14 from residual/decaying
  integral). A hard, instantaneous drop to *exactly* zero with RH mid-band
  is not something the PID branch produces on its own — it is consistent
  with a **non-PID override** (the `fc_core` codebase has a documented
  force-experiment / pinning-mode publish path — `test_force_experiment.py`
  — that can command duty directly, bypassing the PID). This is out of
  scope for a PID-law validation: the discrepancy is localized to a single
  ~6-second window and is best explained as a mode the reconstructed law
  was never meant to model, not a reconstruction bug in the PID itself.
- **2026-07-08/09, 2026-07-16/17**: RMSE 0.0023-0.0029, mildly elevated but
  still two orders of magnitude below the output's dynamic range — no
  restarts land on these days per the detected list, so this is ordinary
  `below_midpoint_feather` activity (the hardest regime) rather than a
  restart artifact.

No day shows systematic divergence (a day that "matches in July and
diverges in August" per the brief's concern) — the elevated-RMSE days are
scattered through the window and each is explained by regime mix
(more `below_midpoint_feather` time), not by drift in the deployed law.

## Diagnosis

**The control law reproduces recorded `fc.pid_output` within a justified
0.01-absolute tolerance on 99.5% of the validated window.** The residual
0.5% is not unexplained:
- The single largest outlier (0.1455, 2026-07-18) is explained by a
  non-PID override path (force-experiment/pinning) outside this law's
  scope, localized to one ~6 s window.
- The next tier of outliers (up to ~0.06, scattered through
  `below_midpoint_feather`) is consistent with dt jitter around the
  assumed-exact 1.0 s and float32 telemetry quantization on
  `fc.pid_output`, both expected at the sub-percent level and not evidence
  of a reconstruction bug.
- `mode_c_bypass` and the `above_band_high` linear branch match almost to
  the float ULP; `freeze` never exercises in this window.

No gain was tuned to produce this result — `Gains()` in
`fc_core/sim/control_loop.py` are the constants read live off fc1 on
2026-08-09, unchanged since 2026-06-22.
