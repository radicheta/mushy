Do these models know when the chamber moves?

Same afternoon as before — 1 September, an open-loop probe day no fit has seen, models trained on three weeks of August — but scored on the thing that actually matters. Predicting the humidity level five minutes out lets a model bank the level it was handed and coast. Predicting the change takes that away: standing still is now zero, not a respectable score. alice barely registers the movement. frank and irma are the only two that do.

The afternoon

Measured humidity, and the relay pulses driving it. On an ordinary day the relay reacts to RH, so a model can score well by echoing the present; here it ran to a schedule, which is what makes the humidifier response identifiable at all.

Predicted change against actual change

Each dot is one 5-minute window: how much the chamber actually moved (across) against how much the model said it would (up). Dots on the dashed diagonal are perfect. A flat horizontal cloud means the model is predicting roughly nothing whatever the chamber does — and the horizontal axis itself is the “no change” baseline, so a cloud lying along it has added nothing.

Why “low slope” is not a calibration bug

It is tempting to read alice's flat cloud as under-confidence — right shape, wrong size, fixable by multiplying its output. It is not. Rescaling each model's change by its own optimal factor moves alice from 0.949 to 0.947 and frank from 0.794 to 0.793: nothing. A predictor that only weakly knows when the chamber moves should shrink toward zero — that is the correct hedge, not an error. The low slope is a symptom of the low correlation, and only a better correlation fixes it.

Numbers

This afternoon alongside the full August test set, because one forced afternoon is kinder to the physics models than the held-out days are — alice manages r 0.31 here and r 0.05 across August.

Why irving is the fourth

irving is alice plus a wall-moisture state — a strict superset, where kw→0 recovers alice exactly. It answers whether giving the model memory helps it anticipate movement. Across August: r 0.08 against alice's 0.05. It does not.

The baseline that is not on this chart

Scored on absolute humidity instead of RH, a baseline that holds humidity constant and only does the temperature arithmetic beats alice outright (skill 0.679 vs 0.689). Temperature is handed to every model, so much of an AH change is derivable rather than predicted. That is why this page scores RH.

Reading skill

Error in the predicted change, divided by the size of the actual change. Below 1 beats predicting no change; above 1 is worse than standing still. Correlation r is the honest headline — it is what a better structure would have to move.
